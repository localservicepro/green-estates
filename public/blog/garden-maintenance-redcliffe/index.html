Garden maintenance on the Redcliffe peninsula

A scheduled visit in Redcliffe covers the lawn mown and edged, hedges trimmed when they are due, beds weeded, spent growth and palm fronds removed, paths and patios blown down, and a note of anything that needs attention. Because the soil here is sand, we push mulch harder than we do inland: a 75 millimetre layer of hardwood or forest mulch every spring is the single biggest thing that keeps a Redcliffe bed alive through October and November before the storms arrive.

Hedges and screens that cope with salt

The peninsula's best hedges are the tough ones: westringia, coastal rosemary, lilly pilly 'Resilience' in sheltered spots, murraya, and the bougainvillea that spills over half the fences in Margate and Woody Point. Each is pruned differently. Westringia is tipped little and often and never cut into bare wood; lilly pilly and murraya get a main cut in spring and light trims in summer and autumn; bougainvillea is cut back hard after flowering with gloves and a trailer. Salt-burnt tips on the exposed side of a hedge along Hornibrook Esplanade or the Scarborough foreshore are normal and are trimmed off as new growth comes through.

Established gardens and old trees

Redcliffe's older blocks carry mature frangipanis, poincianas, palms and citrus that need seasonal attention: fronds and seed pods cleared, low limbs lifted off paths and roofs, dead wood removed, and beds under the canopy mulched rather than left to bare sand and weeds. Minor tree work is done on the same visit where it can be reached from the ground or a step ladder; larger work is referred to an arborist.

Frequently asked questions about garden maintenance in Redcliffe

What does garden maintenance cost in Redcliffe?

It depends on the size of the garden, the hedges and how often you want us. A monthly round for a standard peninsula block covering mowing, hedges as due, bed weeding, frond removal and blow-down is quoted as one fixed price per visit; spring mulching is quoted by the cubic metre. Send photos or the address for a fixed quote.

When should hedges be trimmed in Redcliffe?

Main shaping cuts in early spring, light trims in mid-summer and autumn for lilly pilly, murraya and viburnum; little-and-often tipping for westringia and coastal rosemary; a hard cut after flowering for bougainvillea. On exposed peninsula blocks, salt-burnt tips are trimmed off as new growth comes through rather than all at once.

Do you maintain gardens for unit complexes and holiday rentals in Redcliffe?

Yes. Body corporates from Woody Point to Scarborough and holiday-rental owners near the jetty and Suttons Beach are a large part of our Redcliffe round. Shared gardens, hedges and lawns are maintained on a set day with one fixed price per visit, one invoice and photos after each visit.

Why does my Redcliffe garden dry out so fast?

Because the peninsula is sand, which drains in minutes and holds little water or nutrient. A 75 millimetre layer of mulch every spring, deep watering twice a week rather than a daily sprinkle, and a slow-release feed in spring and autumn make the biggest difference. We include mulching and feeding in a maintenance round on request.
