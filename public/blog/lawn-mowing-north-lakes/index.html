Lawn mowing in North Lakes: small blocks, high standards

A typical North Lakes block is 350 to 600 square metres with a front lawn, a strip down the side and a back yard that has to share space with a patio, a pool or a trampoline. It is push-mower territory, and the details are what separate a good job from an average one: edges cut cleanly along the exposed-aggregate driveway, the line around the letterbox and the street tree, no clippings on the footpath, and the buffalo left long enough to stay thick. Sir Walter, Palmetto and Sapphire dominate here, and they are all damaged by being cut short, so we run the deck at 50 to 60 millimetres through summer.

Lake Eden, the Westfield precinct and the newer stages towards Mango Hill and Capestone all share the same character, and the same watering restrictions apply, so we also keep an eye on lawn grub and fungal patches in February, which show up first on the shaded south side of two-storey homes.

How short should buffalo lawn be cut in North Lakes?

Not short. Soft-leaf buffalo such as Sir Walter, Palmetto and Sapphire should be kept at 50 to 60 millimetres in summer and a notch lower in winter. Cutting buffalo short opens the canopy, lets weeds in and browns the lawn in a North Lakes summer. We set the deck height for the grass, not the calendar.

Yes. Common-area lawns, nature strips, hedges and garden beds at townhouse complexes across North Lakes and Mango Hill are handled on a scheduled round with a single fixed price per visit, one invoice and photos after each visit for the committee or manager.

Can you take care of the hedges and garden beds too?

Yes. Most North Lakes clients bundle hedge trimming, garden bed weeding and a spring mulch into their mowing round so there is one visit and one invoice. Lilly pilly and murraya boundary screens are trimmed two to three times a year, timed to the growth flushes.
